Aura.

An Android app for tracking the menstrual cycle and estimating, honestly, what comes next. Health data stays on the phone.

In one minute

Log, estimate, and never show more than is known

Aura stores bleeding days, flow, mood, symptoms and notes. From that it estimates the next period, the current phase and, when there is enough data, ovulation and the fertile window.

It has no account, ads or analytics. The only automatic way data leaves the phone is an optional backup to the user's own Google Drive, always encrypted. Beyond that, only what she shares leaves: a backup file or a PDF summary.

Health notice. Aura offers informational estimates. It is not a method of birth control or a medical device.
Why it exists

Motivation

Aura began as a project by Sofia Menzel, a junior IT graduate, to have a cycle app she would want to use herself and, at the same time, a portfolio piece that shows a complete engineering process.

The first version was built with Flutter and Hive and was left unfinished. It was restarted on a different foundation: SQLite with drift, a functional specification written before coding, and tests from the start.

Version 1.1 came from the author's own usage notes: logging the period without marking day by day, clearer statistics and an explicit way to close the period.

Three convictions guide the design

  • A cycle is health data. That's why the starting rule is that data doesn't leave the phone unless the user decides so.
  • A prediction is an estimate. Aura shows a range and a confidence level, and stays silent when data is missing instead of inventing precision.
  • What is logged and what is estimated don't mix. An estimated day is never saved as if it were real.
For the people who use it

What Aura does

Logging

A full day

Bleeding, flow intensity, mood, eight symptoms and notes of up to 2000 characters. Saving an empty day writes nothing.

Logging

“My period started today”

One tap marks the first day. The following days are shown as estimated and are only saved if she confirms them.

Calendar

Ranges and undo

Mark several days at once, lengthen or shorten a range, and “Undo” on every action. Estimated days have a dotted border, logged days are filled.

Prediction

With range and confidence

Next period, current phase and, when there is data, ovulation and the fertile window. Showing any of it is optional.

Stats

Your cycles

Typical length, shortest and longest, regularity, most frequent symptoms and moods. With fewer than two cycles, it says so instead of averaging badly.

Reminders

Discreet reminders

Off by default. The text on the lock screen is generic, unless “Show details in notifications” is turned on.

Backup

A file, with a password if she wants

Export and import to a file that is validated before anything is touched, with optional encryption and “Undo” after importing.

Backup

Automatic, to Drive

Optional and off by default. One encrypted file that is replaced once a day, like WhatsApp's backup.

Privacy

Hidden in Recents

The preview is blank and screenshots and screen recordings are blocked. It can be turned off.

In the next Google Play update

Already on the main branch, but not in version 1.2.0 published in closed testing.

Pregnancy

Pregnancy mode

Weeks, days and an estimated due date calculated from the last period. Pauses predictions, the fertile window and cycle reminders. When it ends, the outcome is chosen without celebratory wording, and the last pregnancy can be edited or deleted.

Stats

PDF summary

Generated on the phone, with no server, with the last 12 cycles, symptoms, mood, flow, pregnancies and birth control. Shared through Android's share sheet.

Stats

Redesigned screen

Summary cards, a bar chart of recent cycles with the typical length, short explanations and moods as bars.

Widget

Home screen

Next period, day of the period, days late or weeks of pregnancy. With “Discreet widget” it shows no data. It updates to the new day without opening the app.

Reminders

Birth control and daily reminder

A pill, patch, ring or injection reminder, stored only on the phone and left out of backups. A daily reminder if the day has nothing logged. Both optional.

Interface

English and dark theme

The interface follows the phone's language. System default, light or dark theme. Edge-to-edge screens and no orientation lock.

What Aura doesn't do yet

iOS, encryption of the database on the phone, a birth control history (designed, not built), and its own account or sync.

Business rules

How it calculates

Aura uses the calendar method. These are documented product decisions, not clinical criteria, and they live together in a single place in the code (PredictionConfig).

ConceptRule
New periodA bleeding day more than 7 days after the previous one
Valid cycleFrom 15 to 60 days; the rest don't count
Cycles averagedThe 6 most recent, with more weight on the latest
Next period rangeThe larger of 1.5 σ and 2 days; 3 days with fewer than two cycles
ConfidenceLow with fewer than 2 cycles or high spread; medium with 2 or 3 stable cycles; high from 4 stable cycles
OvulationExpected next period minus 14 days
Fertile windowFrom 5 days before ovulation up to ovulation. Only with medium or high confidence and no delay
Out-of-date dataMore than 60 days since the last start: Aura stops predicting
PregnancyPauses prediction until a new period is logged after the end date. Cycles that span a pregnancy don't count toward the prediction, the chart or the PDF

The principle behind everything

Only what the user logged is stored: bleeding days and their details, and the pregnancies she enters. Everything else is calculated each time, so correcting a single day recalculates the rest without contradictions.

How it's built

Architecture

An Android-only Flutter app, organized in layers so the cycle rules can be tested without a phone, without a database and without a real clock.

PresentationScreens and dialogs: Home, Calendar, Log day, Stats and Settings. They react to data streams and contain no cycle rules.
Services and dataRepository, SQLite database with drift, backup, Drive backup, reminder reconciler and state files with atomic writes.
Pure domainFunctions with no Flutter or database: deriving cycles, predicting, deciding what is shown, planning reminders, encoding and encrypting backups.
Outside our own code
  • Android Keystore: seals the automatic backup password
  • Google Drive API: only with automatic backup turned on
  • System notifications
  • Home screen widget and Android share sheet

Robust by design

  • The database opens before anything else. If it fails, the app shows a screen with “Try again” instead of freezing.
  • Each migration runs in a transaction with checks and a prior copy of the file.
  • Argon2id runs on a separate thread so the interface doesn't freeze. Measured: between 0.42 and 0.50 s on a POCO X6 Pro.
  • Injectable clock and database, to test day changes and migrations with fixed dates.

Privacy and permissions

PermissionWhat for
POST_NOTIFICATIONSShowing reminders, only if she turns them on

It doesn't ask for location, contacts, camera, microphone or broad storage access.

What leaves the phone, and when

  • Nothing, as long as there is no backup.
  • Manual backup: a file she saves or shares, with an optional password.
  • Drive backup: a file that is always encrypted, once a day, only if she turns it on. Google sees that it exists and when it changed, not its contents.
  • PDF summary (next update): generated on the phone and only leaves if she shares it. It contains health data and is not encrypted.
Encryption. Argon2id (19,456 KiB, 2 iterations) and AES-256-GCM with the cryptography library. The file header is authenticated: if someone alters it, it won't decrypt. There are tests with known vectors.
What it doesn't protect. The database on the phone isn't encrypted. A backup without a password is readable text. The automatic backup keeps its password sealed with the Android Keystore, unlike manual backups, where Aura stores none. Anyone who looks at the home screen can read the widget, unless it's in discreet mode.

Android doesn't copy Aura's data to the cloud or when changing phones (allowBackup="false"). The privacy policy is published.

How it's tested

  • Boundary values: 15- and 60-day cycles, 7- and 8-day gaps, 2000-character notes.
  • Fixed dates and an injected clock, including Chile's daylight saving change at midnight.
  • Real backups from earlier versions as test data.
  • Every fix keeps its regression test.

Known gaps

  • No continuous integration or end-to-end tests on a device.
  • Coverage percentage not yet measured.
  • The native Kotlin code is only checked by hand.
  • Low-end phones and TalkBack not tested systematically.
Decision log

Decisions that define the product

Data

Derive, don't store

Only bleeding days and the pregnancies entered are stored. One source of truth: correcting a day recalculates everything. Cost: there is no history of past predictions.

Data

Dates as text, in UTC

A calendar day is not an instant. In Chile the clock change happens at midnight and sometimes that hour doesn't exist; working in UTC removes that whole class of bugs.

Backup

Own, versioned JSON

It isn't a copy of the database. It is fully validated before anything is touched, and an older schema is imported by converting it.

Drive

A single file, like WhatsApp

Simple for the user: one copy, always the latest. Accepted cost: no history and no later verification. The original seven-file design remains documented.

Drive

Least privilege

drive.file scope: Aura only sees the files it creates itself. The user can view and delete them from her Drive.

Robustness

Open the database first

Previously, a failure opening it left the app frozen. Now no reminder is scheduled until the database opens, and there is a retry screen.

Executive summary · one page

Aura at a glance

What it is

Aura is an Android app for tracking the menstrual cycle. It records bleeding days, flow, mood, symptoms and notes, and estimates the next period, the current phase and, when there is enough data, ovulation and the fertile window. It is informational and is not a contraceptive or a medical device.

Why it exists

Built by Sofia Menzel, a junior IT graduate, as an app she wanted to use herself and as a portfolio piece that shows a full engineering process. Cycle data is health data, so the design starts from one rule: data stays on the phone unless the user decides otherwise.

How it works

  • Stores only what the user logs. Cycles, predictions and phases are derived on demand, so fixing one day recalculates everything consistently.
  • Honest predictions. A range and a confidence level, and silence when data is missing or stale.
  • Pure domain layer. The rules are plain functions, testable without a device, a database or a real clock.
  • Optional encrypted backup. Argon2id and AES-256-GCM, to a file or to the user's own Google Drive. No accounts, ads or analytics.

Status

Version 1.2.0 is in closed testing on Google Play. The main branch already adds an English interface, a dark theme, a pregnancy mode, a birth-control reminder, a PDF summary for the doctor, redesigned statistics and a home-screen widget; they arrive in the next Google Play update. Still missing: on-device database encryption and iOS. Known gaps are listed openly: no CI yet, no end-to-end tests on device, and coverage still to be measured.
